# Does the LLM judge beat a trained feature classifier? (2026-11-03)

Question: for the mid-decode settled decision ("same answer if stopped now?"), does Open-Jev reading the trace
tail see anything that a logistic regression over cheap surface features of the same tail cannot?

## Setup

- Bench: `agent/math_agent/settledbench.py` re-run with trace capture (commit 5192048, tag
  `settledbench-math500-s0-n96-traces`, MATH-500 seed 0, 96 draws = 83 problems, Qwen3-8B, budget 4096,
  one A10G each for vLLM and Jev, 39 min). Results on S3 under `ci/5192048.../results/<tag>/settledbench/`
  (rows.jsonl, traces.jsonl with per-token logprobs, summary.json, summary-feat.json).
- Scored rows: the bench's filter (uncut answer right), 325 rows over 61 problems, 297 positive (silent cut
  kept the answer right) and 28 negative.
- Feature judge: `agent/math_agent/settledfeat.py`. 18 features computable in the gateway from the token
  stream alone: log tokens, phrase rates in Jev's 1500-char tail window (backtracking, verification, "?",
  newlines), candidate answers so far (boxed or "the answer is"), repeats of the latest candidate, fraction of
  the trace since the last new candidate, digit and symbol density, and tail logprob stats. Logistic regression
  (standardized, C=0.5, balanced) and a small gradient-boosted tree, out-of-fold with problems grouped,
  10 folds x 10 repeats. Jev, DEER and the probe are scored on exactly the same rows.

Strongest single features: newline rate in the tail (0.71), verification-phrase rate (0.71), number of distinct
candidate answers (0.67), repeats of the latest candidate (0.67). Tail logprob stats alone are at chance
(0.62 LR, 0.47 GBT); the model's own confidence over the last 128 tokens does not say whether the answer
has settled. Text features alone reach 0.67 to 0.68; adding logprobs plus length gets to 0.74.

Jev's two questions swapped places versus the 30-problem bench (there done 0.78 / settled 0.77; here
settled 0.78 / done 0.71), which is a reminder of how wide these intervals are.

## Reading

1. The feature classifier recovers most of Jev's signal: 0.74 vs 0.78, inside each other's intervals, and
   it costs microseconds on the gateway CPU instead of 0.4 to 3.9 s on a second GPU.
2. What is left for Jev is the shallow cut (first quarter of the trace), where most of the harmful cuts live
   and where neither judge is good (0.62 to 0.66). That is the only band where an LLM judge could justify its
   cost, and the margin there is 0.04 on 20 negatives.
3. 28 negatives is still too few to separate 0.74 from 0.78. A second seed (another ~30 negatives) would
   halve the interval. Another MATH-500 seed is cheap (40 min, two g5.xlarge).
4. For the paper: the honest claim is "an online classifier on the trace tail predicts a free cut at AUC
   about 0.75; a 2B LLM judge is within noise of it at 1000x the cost." Jev becomes an ablation, with the
   shallow-cut band as the place to look if the team wants to argue for it.

## Floor on the old 30-problem bench

Any trained model collapses on 10 negatives: LR on cut + level + DEER = 0.45 out of fold, and even LR
handed Jev's own score as a feature only reached 0.59 (jev_done raw 0.78). The old bench could not adjudicate
a trained judge; the 96-draw re-run exists for that reason.

## End to end: Jev on both jobs vs word model + feature judge (same day)

Robert's framing: run both sides at once against the served model's accuracy. Side A = Jev as the budget
predictor (bucket classification) and as the mid-decode settled judge, on its own GPU. Side B = the online
word model as the budget predictor and the trained feature classifier (calibrated LR, `budget_classifier/
weights.json`, fitted on the seed-0 bench above) as the judge, inline on the gateway CPU. Each side gets the
same boxes; side B simply leaves the Jev GPU idle.

Commit 969bc1b: `--judge {jev,feat}` on the gateway, admin key `judge`, sweep spec token `jjev` / `jfeat`;
the feature judge asks vLLM for token logprobs (never forwarded to the client) and scores in about 1 ms.
Exit threshold 0.98 for the feature judge: its probabilities are calibrated against a 91% positive rate, and
on the bench 0.98 exits about as many problems as Jev at 0.7 (45 vs 43 of 61) at close to the same mean
thinking, so both sides shed comparably.

Reading:

1. The 11-02 Jev-as-predictor lead did not repeat. Same seed, same problems, same boxes: 11-02 had Jev 0.898
   against the word model 0.816; today Jev buckets 0.816 against the word model 0.837, 2 gained and 3 lost.
   `none` itself moved from 0.796 to 0.837. The 11-02 writeup said the gap was as likely noise; this run says so.
2. The Jev judge arm is the best arm, 0.878, with 5 gained and 1 lost against `none` and 3 gained, 0 lost
   against plain Jev buckets. But it is not a free win: settled mode cut only 4 + 62 requests against 61 + 97
   for plain Jev buckets, so it kept 4013 thinking tokens per workflow against 2893, and p95 latency was 421 s
   against 221 s. Part of its accuracy is simply thinking longer on level 5 (6742 against 3753). Deadlines
   still met 98 percent, so at this load that was affordable.
3. The feature judge arm is the worst arm, 0.796, 2 gained and 4 lost against the word model alone. It exited
   only 11 workflows (mean settled 0.86 against a 0.98 threshold) and shed the most thinking, 2515 per
   workflow. The weights were fitted on Qwen3-8B traces; on Qwen3-4B they fire rarely and the ranked cuts under
   them land on the wrong requests. This arm needs weights fitted on the served model before it is a fair
   comparison.
4. Every brownout arm still met 98 to 100 percent of deadlines and cut p50 latency from 418 s to 123 to 161 s.
   That is the result that holds across every run.
5. The spike is still one seed and 49 problems. No comparison here reaches p < 0.2. Two more seeds of this
   five-arm sweep, about 65 min and $3 each, are the next step; and refitting the feature weights on Qwen3-4B
   traces before the next one.
