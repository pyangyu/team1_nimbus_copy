# Jev as the settled judge: where Jev can work in Thinking Brownout (2026-10-29, overnight)

## The question

The 10-28 shed-depth sweep closed the budget-prediction door on MATH-500: with the shed cap at 0.7, uniform
cutting keeps the uncut accuracy, and an oracle that knows every request's needed budget in advance recovers
nothing more. A prompt-level predictor (Jev or the word model) cannot show a difference there.

What the 10-28 label bench did show is that a per-request "settled" signal is worth something: a probe that
said the answer was already fixed raised the chance that a cut was free from about 0.7 to about 0.9. The
probe is a trial answer from the serving model itself, one more request at the back of the overloaded queue
(eight minutes of wait in the ceiling sweep). Jev is prefill-only on its own GPU and answers in well under a
second. So the question tonight: can Jev, re-asked mid-decode with the thinking so far, tell which running
requests can be cut for free? If so, that is a job Jev can do in Brownout that nothing else in the system
can do at the moment of the cut.

Three pieces were built and run, in order: an offline bench that scores Jev as a settled detector against
what a real cut does; a gateway cut mode that uses the verdict; and a mixed workload (GSM8K, MATH-500 and
AIME in one stream, BurstGPT arrival timing) to see whether any predictor has room once needs differ widely.

## 1. The settled-detector bench

`agent/math_agent/settledbench.py` (`infra/brownout/settledbench.sh` runs it on the vLLM box and the Jev
box). For each of the 30 distinct MATH-500 level 4 to 5 problems the seed-0 sweep draws: plan, the agent's
solve prompt, the uncut thinking (greedy, budget 4096). Then at every cut point below the length (bucket
edges 256 to 2048 and 25, 50, 75 and 90 percent of the trace, deduplicated) three judges and one ground
truth, all on the same partial trace:

- **Jev** (Open-Jev-2B, untrained, zero-shot) reads the prompt head and the last 1,500 characters of the
  thinking and answers two questions: a four-level score "how far along is the thinking toward a fixed final
  answer" (`settled` = mass on the top two levels) and a yes/no "if stopped here, would the model give the
  same answer as with unlimited thinking" (`done`).
- **DEER**: the serving model's own confidence in a trial answer (close the thinking, prefill the final-answer
  prefix, mean token probability of the answer it writes). One vLLM call.
- **Probe**: the gateway's needed-budget label, the same trial answer compared with the answer at the full
  thinking. One vLLM call, and it needs the full trace, so it only works offline.
- **Ground truth**: a silent cut at that point, early-stop line, full 1,024-token answer, graded.

155 rows, 30 problems, 430 vLLM calls, 240K completion tokens, 24 minutes. Tag
`settledbench-math500-s0` on commit 1679451. Uncut accuracy 0.667 (20 of 30); 14 of 30 traces closed on
their own, 16 hit the budget.

### Which judge predicts that a cut is free

AUC against "the silent cut kept the answer right", on the 105 rows whose uncut answer is correct:

| Judge | AUC | Cost per judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Jev `done` (yes/no) | **0.777** | 0.42 s p50 on the Jev box, 8 in flight, no serving-model time |
| Jev `settled` (score) | 0.766 | same call |
| Probe (needed-budget label) | 0.755 | one vLLM call, plus the full trace to compare against |
| DEER confidence | 0.646 | one vLLM call |

Calibration of Jev `done` on those rows:

| Jev `done` | Rows | Cut kept the answer right | Cut gave the same answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| below 0.5 | 30 | 0.77 | 0.70 |
| 0.5 to 0.7 | 31 | 0.90 | 0.77 |
| 0.7 to 0.8 | 19 | 1.00 | 0.95 |
| 0.8 and up | 25 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

Above 0.7, every one of 44 rows survived the cut (precision 1.00), and those rows hold 24 percent of all the
thinking tokens that could have been cut. At 0.6 the precision is 0.98 for a third of the tokens. DEER's
confidence is above 0.9 on 75 of the 105 rows, including at a quarter of the trace, and says nothing useful
below that; the model is as sure of a wrong early answer as of a right late one.

What the judge is reading. Traces that closed on their own are always safe to cut in this sample (75 of
75 rows kept the answer) and get a mean `done` of 0.68; traces that hit the budget keep the answer 0.67 of
the time and get 0.48. Within the budget-hitting traces alone the AUC of `done` is 0.54, and within the
first quarter of a trace it is 0.71 (37 rows, 7 of them harmful cuts). So most of the signal is telling a
converging trace from a floundering one, not finding the safe point inside a floundering one. That is still
the decision that matters at a cut: a converging trace can be cut anywhere, a floundering one should be
left alone or cut last, and the judge sorts the two while they run.

### Early exit, simulated

Per problem, exit at the first cut point whose signal clears the threshold, otherwise run uncut. Accuracy
and mean thinking tokens over the 30 problems (uncut: 0.667, 3,292 tokens):

| Policy | Accuracy | Mean thinking | Exited |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.667 | 3,292 | 0 |
| oracle (earliest free cut) | 0.700 | 1,795 | 21 |
| Jev `done` at 0.7 | **0.667** | **2,566** | 18 |
| Jev `done` at 0.8 | 0.667 | 2,971 | 15 |
| Jev `done` at 0.6 | 0.633 | 2,031 | 23 |
| Jev `settled` at 0.8 | 0.667 | 3,175 | 9 |
| DEER at 0.95 | 0.633 | 2,463 | 12 |
| DEER at 0.9 | 0.567 | 1,630 | 23 |
| probe label (not causal) | 0.667 | 1,739 | 27 |

Jev `done` at 0.7 saves 22 percent of the thinking at the uncut accuracy, zero-shot, from a 2B model that
never saw a thinking trace in training. The oracle saves 45 percent. DEER cannot get to the uncut accuracy
at any threshold that exits more than a handful of problems.

So the gateway's settled mode uses `done` as P(settled) and 0.7 as the exit threshold.

## 2. The settled cut mode in the gateway

Commit 743d0ff. `gateway/brownout_gateway/judge.py` is a latest-wins queue: from 256 thinking tokens and
every 512 after, a streaming request is handed to Jev with its prompt head and the tail of what it has
streamed; the answer lands on the request as `settled`. Cut mode `settled` is the ranked controller with one
change: a request's remaining thinking is priced at (1 - settled) of its prompt-level cost, so a request Jev
judges settled is the cheapest to cut and goes first, down to what it has used. `--exit-at` (sweep spec
`e<tau>`) cuts a request outright once P(settled) clears the threshold while the server is under pressure.
Everything is logged per judgment (`judge` rows in the gateway log) and per request (`settled`, `judged_at`,
`judgments`), and `/v1/stats` carries judge counters. 51 gateway unit tests.

Paired against that seed's `none`: uniform lost 3 and gained 0 on seed 0, lost 4 and gained 1 on seed 1;
settled with exit gained 2 and lost 1 on seed 0, lost 4 and gained 1 on seed 1. Everything is inside the
three-workflow noise band of a 32-workflow run. Accuracy does not separate the policies here, as the 10-28
ceiling result said it would not.

Two things the sweep did settle.

**The ranked family under-sheds, and it is the ledger bank.** Every ranked and settled run kept 4,100 to
4,400 thinking tokens per workflow against uniform's 2,900 to 3,000, and missed 12 to 22 percent of
deadlines where uniform missed 0 to 9. The bank added on 10-28 (commit c9f15fa) credits a finished request
with what it gave up beyond its proportional share; a request cut to its floor banks about 900 tokens, and
under sustained pressure ten such requests bank three whole shares, so later arrivals are never cut until
the pressure lapses. The bank is now off by default (commit 049f05f, `--bank` restores it) and the runs
below repeat without it.

**The judge's verdict separates free cuts from harmful ones, live.** Joining the gateway log with each
workflow's outcome, for the solve calls the controller cut:

| Run | Cut with verdict at or above 0.7 | ... workflow correct | Cut with verdict below 0.7 | ... workflow correct |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| settled, seed 0 | 18 | 0.83 | 23 | 0.35 |
| settled with exit, seed 0 | 24 | 0.92 | 16 | 0.31 |
| settled, seed 1 | 17 | 1.00 | 20 | 0.50 |
| settled with exit, seed 1 | 21 | 0.95 | 18 | 0.22 |

74 of the 80 solve calls cut on a verdict of 0.7 or more ended in a correct workflow, after a mean of about
1,700 thinking tokens against a budget of 4,400 or more. The calls cut below 0.7 ended correct 22 to 50
percent of the time. Part of this is that easy problems settle early, but that is the point: the judge
finds them while they run, which no prompt-level prediction did. Judgments cost 0.55 s at the median and
2.1 s at the 90th percentile under load (the two Jev servers also answer the prompt-level question for
every request), and never touch the serving model. 1,066 judgments over the four settled runs, no failures.

## 3. The mixed workload with BurstGPT timing

`--dataset mixed` draws GSM8K, MATH-500 (levels 4 to 5) and AIME 2026 at weights 0.5, 0.35 and 0.15, with a
thinking budget per source (1,024, 4,096 and 6,144; AIME asked for 8,192 in the first attempt and every
AIME call failed against vLLM's 8K context) and a solo latency per source for the deadlines (60, 240 and
600 s, slack 1.5 to 6). 48 workflows at a mean of 2/s with BurstGPT arrival timing (gap coefficient of
variation 0.91 for this seed), judge from 128 tokens and every 256 after, bank off, seed 0.

with 10 instant failures. On those 38:

Commit 71a89b4, seed 0. 19 GSM8K, 19 MATH-500 and 10 AIME workflows, 48 at a mean of 2/s with BurstGPT
timing. 4 runs, 65 minutes with the cap-0.9 pair.

| Policy | Accuracy | Deadlines met | Correct and on time | Mean thinking | GSM8K on time | MATH on time | AIME correct | Cuts | Exits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.771 | 0.21 | 0.104 | 5104 | 0.00 | 0.21 | 1 of 10 | 0 | |
| uniform (cap 0.7) | 0.771 | 0.81 | 0.625 | 2255 | 0.79 | 0.74 | 1 of 10 | 60 | |
| Jev settled, exit at 0.7 | 0.750 | 0.85 | 0.625 | 2394 | 0.79 | 0.84 | 0 of 10 | 70 | 27 |
| Jev ranked (prompt-level) | 0.750 | 0.75 | 0.542 | 2695 | 0.58 | 0.79 | 0 of 10 | 65 | |

Uncut, the ten AIME workflows think for 12,000 tokens each (6,144 plus a retry) and nothing else gets
through: no GSM8K deadline is met and a fifth of the MATH ones. Any cutting policy at cap 0.7 fixes most of
that; uniform and the judge tie at 0.625 correct and on time, and neither loses a workflow that uncut got
right. Qwen3-8B gets 0 or 1 of the 10 AIME problems right at any budget, so the AIME traffic is pure load
with no accuracy at stake. The prompt-level Jev (ranked) is the worst of the three on deadlines, because
untrained Jev's budget guesses spread the cut unevenly.

What the judge saw, per source, at the moment each solve call was cut:

| Source | Calls cut | Mean verdict at the cut | Workflow correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| GSM8K | 16 | 0.77 | 1.00 |
| MATH-500 | 27 | 0.62 | 0.85 |
| AIME 2026 | 20 | 0.36 | 0.00 |

From the running trace alone, with no knowledge of which dataset a request came from, the judge sorts the
three sources in the right order: GSM8K requests are settled early, MATH requests mostly, AIME requests
not at all. The 29 calls cut on a verdict at or above 0.7 ended correct 90 percent of the time after 1,100
tokens; the 35 cut below 0.7 ended correct 40 percent of the time after 1,900.

That also shows the limit of "settled" as the cut criterion. A request that is not settled is either
converging slowly (cut it last) or hopeless (cut it first); the AIME requests here are the second kind, and
the settled policy protects them, spending capacity on thinking that will not produce a right answer. The
verdict the controller needs for that case is "will this request get there at all", which is a different
question to Jev, and one the same mid-decode mechanism can ask.

Judgments in this run cost 1.2 s at the median and 3.9 s at the 90th percentile: 48 workflows judged every
256 tokens saturate two Open-Jev servers that also answer the prompt-level question. The judge should ask
only at bucket edges, or drop the prompt-level call once it is judging.

### BurstGPT, measured

`agent/math_agent/datasets/burstgpt.py` replays a window of the BurstGPT trace (HPMLL/BurstGPT, Azure
OpenAI traffic at a campus; `BurstGPT_1.csv`, first 173,602 requests downloaded). The trace's first 225
hours are near-Poisson at well under 0.1 requests per second (variance-to-mean of the per-second counts 0.96
to 1.08 every hour). From hour 226 the rate climbs to 3 to 9 per second with variance-to-mean of 5 to 9 in
some hours. The package ships the per-second counts of that stretch (49,006 seconds, 145,067 arrivals,
105 KB). The load generator takes n consecutive arrivals from a seeded offset, jitters each within its
second, and rescales time so the mean rate is the requested one.

At the scale of one sweep run this gives real timing with no extra burst: the gaps between 32 to 64
consecutive arrivals have a coefficient of variation of 1.0 on average (Poisson is 1.0) and 1.3 to 1.4 at the
90th percentile. BurstGPT's burstiness is in how the rate moves over minutes and hours, which a 30-second run
of 32 workflows cannot see. A run long enough to cross a rate change (hundreds of workflows) would.

The same-night `none` on these problems scored 0.688.

At the harsh cap uniform loses five workflows against uncut; the judge-driven cut loses three, keeps 18

## The answer to the night's question

**How can Jev work in Thinking Brownout?** As the judge at the moment of the cut. Prompt-level prediction has no room on any workload tried (10-28 ceiling, and the
mixed stream above, where prompt-level Jev is the worst policy on deadlines). Mid-decode, reading the
trace so far, untrained Jev tells converging requests from floundering ones with an AUC of 0.78 offline,
beats the serving model's own confidence (0.65), costs no serving-model time, and in the live gateway its
verdict at the cut separated calls that ended correct (74 of 80 above 0.7 on MATH-500, 26 of 29 on the
mixed stream) from calls that did not (22 to 50 percent below 0.7).

**How can it show results?** Three ways, in order of how clean the evidence already is:

1. The offline bench: AUC and the early-exit curve (22 percent of thinking saved at uncut accuracy at
   `done` 0.7, oracle 45 percent, DEER unable to reach uncut accuracy). This is a result now, on 30
   problems; 100 problems and GSM8K and AIME rows would make it a figure.
2. The live verdict-at-cut table: for every cut the controller made, the verdict against the workflow's
   outcome. This is a result now, across six runs.
3. End-to-end accuracy against uniform. This only shows where uniform loses, which is the harsh cap or a
   workload where cuts must be deep. At cap 0.7 on MATH-500 or the mixed stream, uniform already keeps the
   uncut accuracy and nothing can beat it; at cap 0.9 the two seeds split (judge ahead by two on seed 0,
   behind by two on seed 1, section 5). Over the four seed-level comparisons of correct-and-on-time, the
   judge is never behind and ahead by one workflow on average. Five to ten seeds are needed for a claim of
   a few points.

**Does the workload need to change?** For end-to-end accuracy, yes, in one of two directions. Either a
deeper cut (cap 0.9, or a tighter deadline at cap 0.7) so that uniform loses something the judge can save,
or a stream where "not settled" splits into "converging" and "hopeless", so that the policy can cut the
hopeless requests first; the AIME traffic above is that case and a yes/no "will this request reach a right
answer" question to Jev is the natural next experiment. Mixing GSM8K, MATH and AIME did what it was meant
to do (needs from 300 to 12,000 tokens in one stream, deadlines that only cutting can meet), and it exposed
that the short requests' misses happen in the queue, before any thinking is cut, which is a scheduling
problem Brownout does not address. BurstGPT timing is in, and at this scale it is Poisson-like; it would matter
for a run long enough to see the rate change, and 48 workflows is too short.

Coding, healthcare or finance workloads would change the judge's job only if their answers are not short
and checkable; the mechanism (Jev reads the trace, says whether the answer is fixed) does not depend on the
domain, but the ground truth for the bench does. A coding stream with unit tests as the grader is the one
of the three with a ground truth as clean as a boxed number.
