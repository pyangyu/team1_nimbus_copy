# Jev as the settled judge: where Jev can work in Thinking Brownout (2026-10-29, overnight)

## The question

The 10-28 shed-depth sweep closed the budget-prediction door on MATH-500: with the shed cap at 0.7, uniform
cutting keeps the uncut accuracy, and an oracle that knows every request's needed budget in advance recovers
nothing more. A prompt-level predictor (Jev or the word model) cannot show a difference there.

What the 10-28 label bench did show is that a per-request "settled" signal is worth something: a probe that
said the answer was already fixed raised the chance that a cut was free from about 0.7 to about 0.9. The
probe is a trial answer from the serving model itself, one more request at the back of the overloaded queue
(eight minutes of wait in the ceiling sweep). Jev is prefill-only on its own GPU and answers in well under a
second. So the question tonight: can Jev, re-asked mid-decode with the thinking so far, tell which running
requests can be cut for free? If so, that is a job Jev can do in Brownout that nothing else in the system
can do at the moment of the cut.

Three pieces were built and run, in order: an offline bench that scores Jev as a settled detector against
what a real cut does; a gateway cut mode that uses the verdict; and a mixed workload (GSM8K, MATH-500 and
AIME in one stream, BurstGPT arrival timing) to see whether any predictor has room once needs differ widely.

## 1. The settled-detector bench

`agent/math_agent/settledbench.py` (`infra/brownout/settledbench.sh` runs it on the vLLM box and the Jev
box). For each of the 30 distinct MATH-500 level 4 to 5 problems the seed-0 sweep draws: plan, the agent's
solve prompt, the uncut thinking (greedy, budget 4096). Then at every cut point below the length (bucket
edges 256 to 2048 and 25, 50, 75 and 90 percent of the trace, deduplicated) three judges and one ground
truth, all on the same partial trace:

- **Jev** (Open-Jev-2B, untrained, zero-shot) reads the prompt head and the last 1,500 characters of the
  thinking and answers two questions: a four-level score "how far along is the thinking toward a fixed final
  answer" (`settled` = mass on the top two levels) and a yes/no "if stopped here, would the model give the
  same answer as with unlimited thinking" (`done`).
- **DEER**: the serving model's own confidence in a trial answer (close the thinking, prefill the final-answer
  prefix, mean token probability of the answer it writes). One vLLM call.
- **Probe**: the gateway's needed-budget label, the same trial answer compared with the answer at the full
  thinking. One vLLM call, and it needs the full trace, so it is a label, not a live signal.
- **Ground truth**: a silent cut at that point, early-stop line, full 1,024-token answer, graded.

155 rows, 30 problems, 430 vLLM calls, 240K completion tokens, 24 minutes. Tag
`settledbench-math500-s0` on commit 1679451. Uncut accuracy 0.667 (20 of 30); 14 of 30 traces closed on
their own, 16 hit the budget.

### Which judge predicts that a cut is free

AUC against "the silent cut kept the answer right", on the 105 rows whose uncut answer is correct:

| Judge | AUC | Cost per judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Jev `done` (yes/no) | **0.777** | 0.42 s p50 on the Jev box, 8 in flight, no serving-model time |
| Jev `settled` (score) | 0.766 | same call |
| Probe (needed-budget label) | 0.755 | one vLLM call, plus the full trace to compare against |
| DEER confidence | 0.646 | one vLLM call |

Calibration of Jev `done` on those rows:

| Jev `done` | Rows | Cut kept the answer right | Cut gave the same answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| below 0.5 | 30 | 0.77 | 0.70 |
| 0.5 to 0.7 | 31 | 0.90 | 0.77 |
| 0.7 to 0.8 | 19 | 1.00 | 0.95 |
| 0.8 and up | 25 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

Above 0.7, every one of 44 rows survived the cut (precision 1.00), and those rows hold 24 percent of all the
thinking tokens that could have been cut. At 0.6 the precision is 0.98 for a third of the tokens. DEER's
confidence is above 0.9 on 75 of the 105 rows, including at a quarter of the trace, and says nothing useful
below that; the model is as sure of a wrong early answer as of a right late one.

Two things the judge is partly reading. Traces that closed on their own are always safe to cut in this
sample (75 of 75 rows kept the answer) and get a mean `done` of 0.68; traces that hit the budget keep the
answer 0.67 of the time and get 0.48. And `done` rises with depth: 0.51 in the first quarter, 0.64 in the
second, 0.70 past half. It is not only depth, though: within each depth band the ordering holds, which is
what the AUC measures.

### Early exit, simulated

Per problem, exit at the first cut point whose signal clears the threshold, otherwise run uncut. Accuracy
and mean thinking tokens over the 30 problems (uncut: 0.667, 3,292 tokens):

| Policy | Accuracy | Mean thinking | Exited |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.667 | 3,292 | 0 |
| oracle (earliest free cut) | 0.700 | 1,795 | 21 |
| Jev `done` at 0.7 | **0.667** | **2,566** | 18 |
| Jev `done` at 0.8 | 0.667 | 2,971 | 15 |
| Jev `done` at 0.6 | 0.633 | 2,031 | 23 |
| Jev `settled` at 0.8 | 0.667 | 3,175 | 9 |
| DEER at 0.95 | 0.633 | 2,463 | 12 |
| DEER at 0.9 | 0.567 | 1,630 | 23 |
| probe label (not causal) | 0.667 | 1,739 | 27 |

Jev `done` at 0.7 saves 22 percent of the thinking at the uncut accuracy, zero-shot, from a 2B model that
never saw a thinking trace in training. The oracle saves 45 percent. DEER cannot get to the uncut accuracy
at any threshold that exits more than a handful of problems.

So the gateway's settled mode uses `done` as P(settled) and 0.7 as the exit threshold.

## 2. The settled cut mode in the gateway

Commit 743d0ff. `gateway/brownout_gateway/judge.py` is a latest-wins queue: from 256 thinking tokens and
every 512 after, a streaming request is handed to Jev with its prompt head and the tail of what it has
streamed; the answer lands on the request as `settled`. Cut mode `settled` is the ranked controller with one
change: a request's remaining thinking is priced at (1 - settled) of its prompt-level cost, so a request Jev
judges settled is the cheapest to cut and goes first, down to what it has used. `--exit-at` (sweep spec
`e<tau>`) cuts a request outright once P(settled) clears the threshold while the server is under pressure.
Everything is logged per judgment (`judge` rows in the gateway log) and per request (`settled`, `judged_at`,
`judgments`), and `/v1/stats` carries judge counters. 51 gateway unit tests.

### BurstGPT, measured

`agent/math_agent/datasets/burstgpt.py` replays a window of the BurstGPT trace (HPMLL/BurstGPT, Azure
OpenAI traffic at a campus; `BurstGPT_1.csv`, first 173,602 requests downloaded). The trace's first 225
hours are near-Poisson at well under 0.1 requests per second (variance-to-mean of the per-second counts 0.96
to 1.08 every hour). From hour 226 the rate climbs to 3 to 9 per second with variance-to-mean of 5 to 9 in
some hours. The package ships the per-second counts of that stretch (49,006 seconds, 145,067 arrivals,
105 KB). The load generator takes n consecutive arrivals from a seeded offset, jitters each within its
second, and rescales time so the mean rate is the requested one.

What this gives at the scale of one sweep run is real timing, not a harsher burst: the gaps between 32 to 64
consecutive arrivals have a coefficient of variation of 1.0 on average (Poisson is 1.0) and 1.3 to 1.4 at the
90th percentile. BurstGPT's burstiness is in how the rate moves over minutes and hours, which a 30-second run
of 32 workflows cannot see. A run long enough to cross a rate change (hundreds of workflows) would.
