# Ranked cuts on needed budgets: the ceiling test (2026-10-28)

## Question

The 10-22 sweep showed that which predictor sets the budget (untrained Jev or the word model) does not
move answer accuracy: every cut policy lands within a workflow or two of `uniform`, and all of them lose
13 to 16 accuracy points against `none` on MATH-500 in exchange for meeting every deadline. The predictor
only set each request's starting budget; the cut itself took the same fraction from everyone.

The idea tested here: predict how much thinking each request *needs* (the smallest budget at which its
answer no longer changes), and under pressure cut the requests already past that number first. Before
training any predictor for it, measure the ceiling with an oracle: the needed budget measured on the same
prompts in an uncut run. If the oracle recovers most of the MATH-500 gap, a predictor has room; if not,
the predictor is not the lever.

## What was built (branch `ranked-cuts`)

- **Needed-budget labels** (`brownout-gateway --labels needed`). When a request ends on its own terms
  (closes its thinking or hits the client's budget), the gateway replays its own thinking cut at the
  bucket edges below its length, closes the thinking with the agent's early-stop line, and asks for the
  final answer only. The label is the lowest edge whose answer still matches the answer at the full
  thinking. Probes walk down from the top edge and stop at the first mismatch. Labels feed the word
  model, Jev's examples and the oracle.
- **Ranked cuts** (`--cut ranked`). The controller sheds the same total as the proportional rule but
  takes it from the cheapest bucket slices first, where a slice of bucket k costs `p_k` times the
  fraction of the bucket removed, using the predictor's probabilities (Jev's, the word model's, or a
  one-hot from the oracle). Mass in buckets the request has passed is dropped; mass past what the
  request asked for folds into its top bucket.
- The live sweep takes `policy:notice:predictor:cut`, waits for probes to land, and reports the
  ranked and probe counters.

## What the probes said

In the `none` run, 70 requests were probed (234 probe calls, no errors). 59 of 70 needed less thinking
than they used. Agreement by edge over the whole sweep: 32 tokens 46/48, 128 48/50, 256 51/61,
512 10/15, 1K 15/28, 2K 26/40, 4K 11/15. The low edges are dominated by verify steps (a confidence
number, which the model produces without thinking). For the 41 solve steps with a label, 34 needed less
than they used, and the needed buckets were 4K: 12, 2K: 10, 1K: 11, 512: 7, 256: 1.

Probes queued behind the burst: about eight minutes mean wait each, since they are ordinary vLLM
requests at the back of the same queue. The sweep waits for them, so the oracle is unaffected, but
in-run learning from these labels would be slow under load.

## Findings

1. **The oracle did not beat uniform.** 0.63 (announced) and 0.56 (silent) against 0.66. With 32
   workflows these are one to three workflows, and `uniform` itself scored 0.66 here against 0.59 on
   10-22, so run-to-run noise is the size of the effects.
2. **The ranked controller shed more than it should have.** Ranked runs cut 59 to 65 of about 70 think
   calls, uniform 43. The ledger drifted: when a hard-cut request finished, its share left the active
   set and the controller took the difference from the requests it had spared. Fixed after this run
   (commit c9f15fa: a bank keeps what each finished request gave up beyond its proportional share).
3. **The needed label did not predict which cuts hurt.** Joining each run's cuts with the oracle's
   labels: in every cut policy about half the limited solve calls were cut below their needed label,
   and none of those were in a workflow that lost correctness. The workflows that did lose were cut at
   or above their label. The probe path (silent stop, final answer only, 48 tokens) and the serving
   path (silent cut then a 1024-token answer, or an announced cut with the gateway notice and a few
   more thinking tokens) are different decoding paths, and the label from one does not transfer to the
   other at this sample size.
4. **Where the accuracy goes.** Only 2 to 5 of the 23 correct workflows flip under any cut policy, and
   the flips are not the requests the label marks as needing their budget. The loss is small, spread
   across requests, and unpredictable from the prompt-level label as measured.

## Next

The label is the weak link, so test it directly and offline before touching predictors again:
`agent/math_agent/labelbench.py` runs the probe beside the real cut paths (silent cut then full
answer; announced cut with the gateway notice or the countdown notice, 64 or 256 more tokens) on the
same thinking traces at every bucket edge, graded against gold. If the probe label predicts survival
on the real paths, the controller (now with the fixed ledger) is worth re-running against uniform with
more seeds. If it does not, the label has to come from the real path (3 to 4 times the probe cost) or
from a different signal, and the ranked-cut idea waits on that.

## Label fidelity bench (same day, tag `labelbench-20261028-1906`, commit 847beec)

`agent/math_agent/labelbench.py` on the vLLM box alone: the 30 distinct MATH-500 problems the seed-0
sweep draws, budget 4096, greedy. For each problem: the uncut thinking trace; then at every bucket edge
below its length, the probe (early stop, final answer only, 48 tokens) and five real paths (silent cut
then a full answer; the gateway notice `<budget_cut left=N>` or the countdown sentence, with N = 64 or
256 more thinking tokens, then a full answer). 173 problem-by-edge rows, 1,850 calls, 804K completion
tokens, 73 minutes. 22 of 30 problems are correct uncut; 16 of 30 hit the 4096 budget.

Other facts from the rows:
- The label is monotone up the edges for 24 of 30 problems.
- Traces that hit the 4096 budget are where cuts hurt: a silent cut keeps the answer right 0.48 of
  the time on those rows against 0.88 on traces that closed on their own.
- The answer phase writes about 730 tokens on every path, cut or not: the model keeps reasoning in
  the answer, which is why a 48-token final-only probe and the real path can disagree.
- The gateway notice is ignored by the untrained model (it closes its thinking on its own in 7 to 9
  percent of cases and spends nearly all of N). The countdown sentence makes it stop at once (97
  percent close within a few tokens), the panic Matthew's training README describes.

### What this changes

The label is useful: a "settled" probe raises the chance a cut is free from about 0.7 to about 0.9.
But depth dominates. A cut to a quarter of the trace costs about 20 points whatever the label says,
and a cut that leaves half or more costs almost nothing. The controller's rule takes 90 percent of
every budget at pressure 1 (`max_frac`), so in the ceiling sweep nearly every cut sat in the top row,
where no ranking can help. Meanwhile `uniform` met every deadline with a p99 latency of 397 s against
a tightest deadline of 450 s: most of that shedding was not needed.

So the next question is the shed depth, not the predictor: with the cap at 0.7 or 0.5 instead of 0.9,
do deadlines still hold, and does oracle-ranked (with the fixed ledger) now beat uniform at the same
cap? Sweep launched as tag `shed-*`: MATH-500, seeds 0 and 1, `none`, `uniform` at 0.9 / 0.7 / 0.5,
oracle-ranked announced at 0.9 / 0.7 / 0.5. The metric is correct and on time.
