# Jev vs the word model under one BurstGPT spike (2026-11-02)

Question: when Jev and the online word model each set the thinking budgets during a real traffic spike, with
the same GPUs behind the LLM, does either keep more workflows correct and on time?

## Setup

- Spike suite (`BROWNOUT_SUITE=spike`, commit eaccf02). BurstGPT conversation log from hour 522.0, a 16-minute
  window of 115 requests replayed in 240 s at load 2.04: 120 s at 0.89 x C, then 120 s at 3.19 x C, 900 s tail.
- Qwen3-4B on one A10G for every arm. Open-Jev-2B (untrained) on the second A10G, used only by the Jev arm,
  so every arm has the same LLM capacity. C = 0.1 workflows/s, 49 workflows per arm.
- MATH-500 levels 1 to 5 from split a, per-level budgets 2048 / 4096 / 6144, deadlines at slack 1.5 / 3 / 6
  times solo latency. Pressure is the queue term plus decode time past 35 ms per output token.
- Every arm starts from a word model warmed on 64 split-b workflows under `none` (snapshot, then warm reset).
  Jev's few-shot examples come from the same memory.
- Arms: `none`, `brownout:announced:online` (word model), `brownout:announced:jev`. Same seed, so the same
  problems arrive at the same times in every arm.
- Results: `infra/brownout/brownout-results/jev-vs-clf-20261102/` (report.html, results.md, per-arm logs);
  S3 `ci/eaccf02.../results/qwen3-4b-spike-jev-vs-clf-20261102-1616/`.

## Result

| arm | correct and on time | accuracy | deadlines met | p50 latency | mean thinking | notices |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.796 | 0.796 | 0.98 | 334 s | 5916 | 0 |
| brownout, announced, word model | 0.816 | 0.816 | 1.00 | 94 s | 2347 | 110 |
| brownout, announced, Jev | **0.898** | 0.898 | 1.00 | 93 s | 2111 | 102 |

Paired by problem (exact McNemar):

| comparison | gained | lost | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| word model vs none | 3 | 2 | 1.00 |
| Jev vs none | 5 | 0 | 0.062 |
| Jev vs word model | 5 | 1 | 0.219 |

By MATH level, Jev's lead is in the hard problems: level 4 0.92 vs 0.77, level 5 0.83 vs 0.67 (13 and 12
problems).

## Reading

1. Both brownout arms meet every deadline and cut median latency from 334 s to about 93 s with 60 percent
   less thinking. That part holds with either predictor.
2. Jev is four workflows ahead of the word model. With 49 problems per arm that is not significant
   (p = 0.22), and `none` moves by about four points between repeats of this suite.
3. This runs against the pooled bucket accuracy of 10-22 to 10-29, where Jev guessed too short 36 percent of
   the time against the word model's 14 percent. This run logs only the word model's bucket accuracy (exact
   0.28 to 0.34, within one 0.66 to 0.74 across arms), so it cannot say whether Jev guessed lengths better
   here; the gap is as likely noise as a better predictor.
4. Next: a second and third seed of this exact suite before claiming a Jev effect.
